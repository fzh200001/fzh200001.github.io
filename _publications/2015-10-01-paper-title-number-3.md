Since 2020, technological self-reliance has become a central objective of China’s industrial policy, pursued through increasingly targeted efforts to reduce dependence on narrowly defined technology-embodying imports. This paper assesses China’s progress toward import substitution over the past decade, combining aggregate trade analysis with commodity-level evidence. At the macro level, capital goods imports declined markedly relative to GDP, suggesting reduced reliance on foreign inputs. At the micro level, we analyze 85 products across 16 technology groups identified in Chinese policy discourse as areas of vulnerability, using monthly HS-8 digit import data through early 2025. Employing a simple growth–volatility classification, we identify sectors exhibiting high-variance stockpiling (notably semiconductors), persistent import dependence (high-tech capital goods), and successful substitution (e.g., aerospace and selected intermediates). These patterns suggest a policy-induced substitution cycle in which initial import surges precede subsequent domestic replacement. Adjusting for stockpiling reveals a substantial decline in capital goods import dependence—from 2.06% to 1.27% of GDP between 2015 and 2024. While preliminary and descriptive, our analysis highlights two implications: first, substitution is likely to impose short-run costs through lower quality and higher-priced inputs; second, sustained reductions in imports alongside export expansion may exacerbate global trade imbalances.
