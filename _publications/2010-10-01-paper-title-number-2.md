Over the past two decades, China and India have intensified policy efforts to position themselves within the global trade of clean energy (CE) goods. China has achieved market dominance across solar photovoltaics, wind, rare earths, and electric vehicles, while India’s attempts to expand domestic CE manufacturing capacity have produced more uneven results. Despite China’s global share exceeding 60% in most CE product categories and India’s limited penetration, recent geopolitical tensions, supply chain diversification imperatives, and India’s industrial policy ambitions have generated debate over the prospects for rebalancing this asymmetry. Nevertheless, India remains deeply reliant on Chinese technologies, components, and investment, particularly in solar PV and batteries, complicating narratives of economic “decoupling.” Given that China and India are respectively the world’s largest and third-largest carbon emitters, with announced net-zero targets for 2060 and 2070, their bilateral CE trade relationship will exert significant influence on global decarbonization trajectories. This report, produced under the ARETIC initiative, provides a systematic characterization of current trade patterns, introduces relevant data sources, and identifies key research questions to inform future scholarship on the political economy of clean energy transitions.
